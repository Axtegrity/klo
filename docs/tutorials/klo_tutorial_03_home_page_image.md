# Tutorial 03: Changing the Home Page Background Image
**Duration estimate:** 8 minutes
**Series:** KLO Admin Video Tutorials

---

## Prerequisites

Before you start this tutorial, you need:
- Admin access (see Tutorial 01)
- A background image ready to use — JPEG or PNG, at least 1920 pixels wide, under 10 MB
- The admin dashboard open at keithlodom.ai/admin

---

## What You Will Learn

By the end of this video, you will know how to open the home page image editor, click a section to change its background photo, upload a new image, preview it across mobile and desktop sizes, and publish the change live.

---

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Step 1 — Open Edit Home Page

There are two ways to reach the image editor. Use whichever is faster for you.

**Option A — From the Overview tab:**
1. Click the **Overview** tab.
2. Look for the button labeled **Edit Home Page** near the top of the page, below the header.
3. Click it.

**Option B — From the address bar:**
1. In your browser address bar, type **keithlodom.ai/admin/edit** and press Enter.

[SHOW: the Edit Home Page button on the Overview tab]
[NARRATE: "Either route takes you to the same place — the home page image editor."]

---

### Step 2 — Read the editor layout

When the editor opens, you will see a sticky bar across the very top of the screen. This bar has:

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| **"Editing Home Page"** label | Confirms you are in the right place |
| **Mobile / Tablet / Desktop** toggle | Changes how wide the preview is — lets you check how the image looks at different screen sizes |
| **Discard** button | Throws away any unpublished changes |
| **Publish** button | Makes your changes go live |
| **Exit** button | Returns you to the admin dashboard |

Below the bar, you will see your actual home page — laid out exactly as visitors see it.

[SHOW: the full editor with sticky bar visible]
[NARRATE: "This is a live preview. Nothing is locked — you can scroll down through the entire home page right here in the editor."]

---

### Step 3 — Find the image you want to change

The home page has three sections with editable background images:

1. **Hero background** — the large photo or color behind the main heading at the top
2. **Latest Brief watermark** — the background image behind the Intelligence Brief card
3. **Featured Insight watermark** — the background image behind the Featured Insight card

To find them, look for a blue glowing outline around a section when you hover over it. Any section with that outline has a clickable image.

[SHOW: cursor hovering over the Hero section, blue outline appearing]
[NARRATE: "When you hover, the blue outline appears and a small label pops up showing what that image slot is called. That is how you know it is clickable."]

---

### Step 4 — Click the image to change it

1. Hover over the section whose image you want to change.
2. Wait for the blue outline and the label to appear.
3. Click anywhere inside that section.

A file picker will open immediately.

[SHOW: the file picker dialog opening]
[NARRATE: "This is your computer's standard file browser. You are not uploading to a third-party service — the image goes directly into the site's storage."]

---

### Step 5 — Select your new image

1. In the file picker, navigate to where your image is saved on your computer.
2. Click the image file to select it.
3. Click **Open** (or **Choose** on a Mac).

The editor will show a brief upload indicator, then your new image will appear in the preview.

[SHOW: the new image loading into the hero section]
[NARRATE: "The preview updates instantly. You can see exactly how it will look before you publish."]
[PAUSE: let the image finish loading]

---

### Step 6 — Check the image at different screen sizes

1. Look at the viewport toggle in the top bar.
2. Click **Mobile** — check that the important part of the image is still visible at phone width.
3. Click **Tablet** — check at medium width.
4. Click **Desktop** — this is the default full-width view.

[SHOW: switching between Mobile and Desktop views]
[NARRATE: "Background images are cropped differently on mobile versus desktop. Always check mobile before you publish — a beautiful desktop photo can look cropped or dark on a phone screen if the subject is near the edges."]

---

### Step 7 — Publish the change

When you are happy with how the image looks:

1. Click the **Publish** button in the top bar.
2. The button label will briefly change to **Published** with a checkmark.
3. Open a new browser tab and go to **keithlodom.ai** to confirm the image is live.

[SHOW: the Publish button turning to "Published" checkmark state]
[NARRATE: "Published means the change is live. Your members will see the new image the next time they load the page."]

---

### Step 8 — Exit the editor

1. Click the **Exit** button in the top bar.
2. You will be returned to the main admin dashboard.

[SHOW: returning to the admin dashboard]

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: I clicked the section but no file picker opened.**
Fix: Some browsers block file pickers if pop-ups are restricted. Check for a blocked pop-up notification in your browser bar and allow it. Chrome and Safari both work reliably for this.

**Problem: The image uploaded but it looks dark or low quality in the preview.**
Fix: The image is being overlaid with a dark transparency layer to keep the text readable on top. This is intentional — your image should be bright and high-contrast so it shows through the overlay. Try a lighter version of the same photo.

**Problem: I see a first-visit tooltip that says "Click any picture to change it."**
Fix: That is normal — it only appears the first time you open the editor. Click **Got it** to dismiss it.

**Problem: The Publish button is greyed out.**
Fix: Publish only becomes active after you have made a change. If you have not clicked any image, there is nothing to publish yet.

**Problem: I want to undo the image change.**
Fix: Click **Discard** in the top bar before publishing. This throws away all unpublished changes and restores the previous image. Once you have published, contact your developer to restore the old image from backup.
